    I am an artificial intelligence researcher who studies how AI is governed, framed, and talked about in Africa and beyond. My work looks at algorithmic bias, the governance of AI, and the way the media shape public ideas about technology. I have co-authored two articles on AI, one on algorithmic bias and Black feminist epistemologies and one on how AI changes historical inquiry. My latest paper examines how African magazine covers frame artificial intelligence and what that framing says about who is expected to govern it. It received an award for paper quality and is now under review in a journal. I presented my AI research at the Texas Tech University Graduate School Poster Competition, and I was invited to speak on artificial intelligence at the United Nations ECOSOC Youth Forum in New York.
    My approach comes from background in the media. I began in broadcast journalism in Ghana, where I hosted a political affairs program at Radio Universe and reported on the 2020 elections for Asaase Radio. I later worked in communications at the Ministry of Information. At Texas Tech University I served as a teaching assistant and as Graduate Vice President of the Student Government Association. I am also President of the Star Africa Network, U.S.A. branch, who mentors students on higher education.
